If you split your year between Southwest Florida and somewhere colder, you know the feeling: you want to walk into your Punta Gorda condo or Venice home and have it simply *work*. 

Getting there takes some planning, especially if you're furnishing from scratch or refreshing a home that has sat through a long, bright summer. 

## Start earlier than you think you need to

Interior Motives Design has access to to-the-trade fabric and furniture lines that are sold only through designers. 

A few practical habits help:

- **Prioritize the pieces you'll use most.** Beds, seating and window treatments usually matter more on day one than accent tables or art.
- **Make decisions in one round where you can.** Going back and forth on selections is one of the easiest ways to push a timeline out.
- **Keep a backup in mind.** If a particular fabric or frame has a long wait, it helps to know your second choice ahead of time.

## Choose fabrics that can handle Florida sun

Southwest Florida light is a big part of why people come here, and it's also hard on interiors. Strong sun through windows and sliders can fade upholstery, rugs and wood finishes, and humidity and salt air near the coast add their own wear. That matters even more in a home that may sit closed up for months at a time.

When selecting upholstery, bedding and drapery, many homeowners in coastal Florida look for:

- **Fade-resistant and performance fabrics** designed to stand up to sunlight and everyday use.
- **Lighter, easy-care textiles** that feel comfortable in warm weather.
- **Fabrics suited to indoor and outdoor spaces** for lanais, porches and pool areas.

This is where seeing the real thing makes a difference. Rather than relying only on mood boards, Interior Motives Design presents selections as real samples laid out in the studio, so you can feel the texture, see the true color and judge how a fabric might look in your home's light. 

## Plan window treatments as protection, not just decoration

For a seasonal home, window treatments do a lot of quiet work. They manage glare when you're in residence and help limit sun exposure on furniture and floors while you're away.

When you're thinking through [window treatments](/services/window-treatments), consider:

- **Layering options**, such as shades for light control paired with drapery for softness.
- **How the treatments will be left when the home is closed up**, so they're protecting your interiors in the off months.

## What the in-home consultation covers

Every project with Interior Motives Design starts with an in-home consultation. It's a chance for the designers to see your space in person and understand how you actually live there during the season.

Typically, that conversation is about:

- **How you use the home**: who visits, whether grandchildren stay over, whether you entertain on the lanai.
- **The light and layout**: sun exposure, views and how rooms flow, which ties into space planning.
- **What stays and what goes**: pieces you love, pieces that have faded or no longer fit.
- **Your priorities and timing**: what needs to be ready first and when you hope to be settled.

## A simple seasonal planning checklist

- Schedule your in-home consultation well ahead of your arrival.
- Decide which rooms and pieces matter most for day one.
- Choose sun-resistant fabrics and plan window treatments for strong-light rooms.
- Approve selections in as few rounds as you can.
- Talk through delivery and placement timing with your designer.
