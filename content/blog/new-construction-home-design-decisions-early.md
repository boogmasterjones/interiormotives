Building a new home in Southwest Florida is exciting, and it comes with a lot of decisions. Many homeowners are surprised by how early the builder needs answers. Long before move-in, someone will ask where the outlets go, which light fixtures you want and what color the cabinets should be.

The good news is that the decisions that matter most are fairly predictable. 

## Why Timing Matters in a New Build

In a finished home, moving a lamp is easy. In a new build, the position of that lamp may depend on where an outlet was placed months earlier, before the drywall went up. Once walls are closed and finishes are installed, changes become harder and more disruptive.

That is why the most useful design work on a new home often happens on paper, before construction moves too far along. At Interior Motives Design, our [new construction design](/services/new-construction-design) work is built around this idea: plan how you will actually live in each room, then make sure the build supports it.

## 1. Plan Furniture Layouts Before Framing

It sounds backward to think about sofas before walls, but furniture layout drives many construction details. Before framing, it helps to know:

- **Where the main seating goes** in the living room, and whether it faces a TV, a view or both
- **Bed placement** in each bedroom, including room for nightstands on both sides
- **Dining table size** and the clearance needed to pull chairs out comfortably
- **How the lanai connects** to indoor living spaces, since outdoor living is a big part of Florida homes

A floor plan can look spacious until you place real furniture in it. Window placement, door swings and walkways can all fight a good layout. Careful [space planning](/services/space-planning) helps you spot those conflicts while there is still time to discuss them with your builder.

## 2. Settle Lighting and Outlet Placement

Lighting and power are among the hardest things to change later, and among the easiest to overlook on a builder's standard plan. Things worth thinking through early:

- **Floor outlets** in living rooms where the sofa floats away from the wall, so lamps don't need cords across the floor
- **Outlets and switches by the bed**, at a height that works with your nightstands
- **Fixture locations centered on furniture**, such as a pendant over the dining table rather than the center of the room
- **Layered lighting**: overhead, task and accent lighting so rooms work day and night
- **Ceiling fan locations**, which matter in Southwest Florida's climate

## 3. Choose a Finish Palette That Works Together

Builders often present selections one category at a time: flooring one day, cabinets another, countertops and tile after that. Each choice can look great on its own and still clash once installed together.

It helps to set an overall palette first, then make individual selections that fit it. A few Florida-specific considerations:

- **Strong natural light** changes how colors read. A white that looks soft in a showroom can feel stark in a bright Gulf Coast room.
- **Flooring choices** should suit sandy feet, pets and humidity as well as your style.
- **Indoor and outdoor finishes** look best when they relate to each other, especially with sliders opening to the lanai.

At Interior Motives Design, selections are presented as real samples laid out in the studio, not just mood boards. 

## 4. Decide on Built-Ins and Feature Walls

Built-ins and feature walls are part of what makes a new home feel custom. They also tend to need planning during construction rather than after. Consider:

- **Media walls and bookcases** that need blocking, power or recessed space
- **Window seats, mudroom benches or a drop zone** near the garage entry
- **Feature walls** with wallpaper, texture or paneling, which may affect lighting choices nearby
- **Kitchen and bath details** like a coffee bar, pantry layout or vanity configuration

Deciding where these go early helps make sure the right support and wiring are in place when they are needed.
